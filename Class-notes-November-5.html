Frequency Distributions and Cross-Tabs

  • A frequency table shows how often each response occurs. A cross-tab shows counts for combinations of two categorical variables.

Scales of Measurement

  • Nominal: Categories with no order, like favorite restaurant.
  • Ordinal: Categories with an order, like low, medium, and high satisfaction.
  • Interval: Equal gaps between values, but no true zero, like temperature in Fahrenheit.
  • Ratio: Equal gaps and a true zero, like dollars spent.

Hypothesis Testing

  • We went over how to choose a test and write the null and alternative hypotheses. The alternative tells us whether the test is one-tailed or two-tailed.
  • Use the test statistic to find the p-value, then compare it to the significance level to decide whether to reject the null.

Descriptive and Inferential Statistics

  • Descriptive stats summarize the data we collected.
  • A one-sample t-test compares a sample mean to a specific value. A chi-square goodness-of-fit test compares observed counts to expected counts.

Slides 23–26

  • 23. Choosing a hypothesis test. Look at the type of data, how many samples there are, and whether the samples are independent or paired. A before-and-after test on the same people is paired.
  • 24. Comparing methods. For a numeric outcome, a t-test can compare two groups. ANOVA compares means across groups, ANCOVA also adjusts for another variable, and regression can show how a numeric predictor relates to the outcome.
  • 25. Multivariate techniques. Dependence methods like regression start with an outcome. Interdependence methods look for patterns, like related variables in factor analysis or similar cases in cluster analysis.
  • 26. Data prep recap. Check and edit responses, code them, and clean up missing or inconsistent values before picking an analysis.
